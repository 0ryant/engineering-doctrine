# ADR 0038: Adopt A Doctrine Content Lifecycle

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Decision date:** 2026-08-06
- **Recorded date:** 2026-08-06
- **Retrospective:** No

## Context

A three-agent audit recorded in [research-doctrine-lifecycle-audit-2026-08.md](../../doctrine/evolution/research-doctrine-lifecycle-audit-2026-08.md) (internal lifecycle-machinery audit, consolidated backlog and drop-candidate sweep, external standards-lifecycle research across IETF, NIST, W3C, CNCF, TC39, OWASP, the Thoughtworks Radar, and MCP SEP-2596) confirmed the corpus's lifecycle is **half-built**:

- The **addition path** is solid and worked: research → evolution note → ADR → layered guidance → navigation → verification ([doctrine-library-change-harness.md](../../doctrine/patterns/doctrine-library-change-harness.md)), with ADR 0007 the clean precedent. But it lacks a new-file-vs-section criterion and any evidence gate on normative promotion.
- The **retirement half does not exist in practice.** Across five releases, zero doctrine files have ever been deleted, renamed, demoted, or superseded — every doctrine file ever created still exists at its original path. The **Deprecation change class** ([doctrine-versioning-and-consumer-compatibility.md](../../doctrine/patterns/doctrine-versioning-and-consumer-compatibility.md) §3) has never been used in any release. No **Superseded** ADR status exists and no ADR has ever been superseded — while [documentation-knowledge.md](../../doctrine/principles/documentation-knowledge.md) demands exactly that discipline of adopters. No file-level status vocabulary, review cadence, or deprecation register exists. The ordinary removal gate (versioning §6: removal only in a major release, with an actively-harmful escape) was never exercised and composes unclearly with §2's 0.y.z sentence pre-1.0.
- The corpus has **diagnosed this about itself twice** without closing it: ADR 0029's residual risk ("The reference corpus can continue to grow unless the change harness enforces singular ownership and control-retirement criteria") and the full-corpus review's "house law §6" finding (the control-lifecycle rule in [normative-language-applicability-and-exceptions.md](../../doctrine/patterns/normative-language-applicability-and-exceptions.md) §6 — owner, review trigger, retirement condition — is demanded of adopters' controls and not applied to the library's own normative debt).
- The **stuck test case**: [feature-flag-lifecycle.md](../../doctrine/patterns/feature-flag-lifecycle.md) is de facto dead (orphaned from the normative link graph, untyped, previously self-contradictory) and no mechanism could mark it so.
- The file owning the lifecycle vocabulary — the versioning pattern — is a single-commit file unrevised since v0.1.0 (2026-04-27) through four subsequent tags, one of six patterns never revised since first landing.

The external research reduced eight mature models to a minimal set that fits a single-operator, pre-1.0 repo, with the known failure mode to design against: *retirement fails wherever it depends on an affirmative act nobody is assigned* (IETF's abandoned annual reviews being the canonical corpse). Source ledger and model-by-model findings live in the evolution note.

Council provenance: three-critic council (doctrine fidelity / correction-class discipline; internal-consistency and source verification; design proportionality and reader impact). 5 blockers and 15 majors resolved in the landed text — among them: the batch's own missing research note (committed as the evolution note above), consumer-facing typed claims relocated out of the maintainer-scoped pattern into versioning §6, the sweep given an explicit release-checklist trigger and a GOVERNANCE reconciliation, restoration added as a first-class move, the inaugural sweep re-scoped to a non-empty cohort, and a factual superlative about the versioning pattern's age corrected.

## Decision

1. **Adopt the lifecycle pattern (new first-class file).** [doctrine-content-lifecycle.md](../../doctrine/patterns/doctrine-content-lifecycle.md) becomes the canonical home for: the status vocabulary (Active by default with no marker; Stub; Deprecated; exits Superseded / Retired / Rescinded; Historical for evolution notes, editorial to apply), the promotion gates (new-file test; evidence expectation), demotion and relocation as ADR-routed moves, deprecation mechanics (replacement-must-be-Active-or-none-required; tag-anchored floor — removal MAY ship in the first minor release cut at least 90 days after the deprecating tag, and MUST NOT ship earlier; expedited rescission for actively harmful text; post-1.0 removals additionally ride a major), never-delete-always-stamp tombstoning with **restoration as a normal ADR-routed move**, the register contract, ADR supersession, and the two anti-accretion engines (release-coupled staleness sweep with cohort bounding; six-month standalone backstop as a maintenance obligation; default-fade with the v0.5.0 tag as the epoch for pre-existing entries). The new-file test is satisfied reflexively: the topic is durable, owned by no existing file, and cross-linked from at least the harness, the versioning pattern, and the ADR index.
2. **Create the register.** [doctrine/DEPRECATED.md](../../doctrine/DEPRECATED.md) — dying table, tombstone table (append-and-annotate only), and sweep ledger, initially empty. Placed at doctrine root beside REFERENCES.md and SEMANTIC_INDEX.md as a meta/navigation surface per the ADR 0004/0011 precedent — it is an index over layers, not a member of one.
3. **Revise the versioning pattern surgically.** (a) §6 "Deprecation And Removal" keeps its number and becomes the **consumer-facing surface**: it states that Deprecated surfaces remain accurate at every tag in which they ship, that new adoption **SHOULD NOT** reference a Deprecated surface, that consumers **SHOULD** stop following Rescinded text even on old pins, that every deprecation carries a replacement (or an explicit none-required) and tag-anchored sunset timing in the register — and routes all mechanics to the lifecycle pattern. (b) §2's 0.y.z sentence gains the clause "including lifecycle-governed removals per [doctrine-content-lifecycle.md](doctrine-content-lifecycle.md)". (c) §3's Deprecation row gains the lifecycle cross-link. (d) §8's checklist gains two lines: `[ ] Lifecycle status markers applied and doctrine/DEPRECATED.md updated for any deprecation, exit, or restoration` and `[ ] Minor release: doctrine-content-lifecycle §8 sweep executed and its record linked from the register's sweep ledger`. No other section changes.
4. **Extend the ADR status vocabulary.** [docs/adr/README.md](README.md) defines **Superseded** (bidirectional links required; a decision disavowed without a successor is reversed by a superseding ADR that records the disavowal) and **Withdrawn** (terminal, proposer-initiated exit for a Proposed ADR; distinct from Rejected). Amendment-in-place remains correct for refinements that do not reverse a decision. A Proposed ADR older than 90 days gets a recorded accept/reject/withdraw decision at the next sweep — [ADR 0021](0021-audit-as-discipline-applies-to-runner-itself.md) (Proposed since 2026-05-20) is the first case, to be decided at the first sweep, not here.
5. **Record the ADR numbering gap.** docs/adr/README.md gains one sentence of verifiable fact: numbers 0013–0020 were never assigned in any reachable history; recorded so the gap is not misread as deleted decisions — consistent with the index's honesty rule. No causal story is asserted.
6. **Hook the harness, checklist, and governance.** [doctrine-library-change-harness.md](../../doctrine/patterns/doctrine-library-change-harness.md) §1 intake gains the question: "Does this change supersede, demote, or retire anything? If yes, route the lifecycle mechanics — banner, register, exit state — in the same change." [doctrine-change-checklist.md](../../doctrine/checklists/doctrine-change-checklist.md) gains the matching line: "Lifecycle: supersession/demotion/retirement routed per doctrine-content-lifecycle.md; DEPRECATED.md updated". [GOVERNANCE.md](../../GOVERNANCE.md)'s release-cadence note gains the clause that the lifecycle §8 sweep is a maintenance obligation, not a release calendar. This applies the V46 enforcement-hook approach to lifecycle changes.
7. **Route the inaugural applications — deliberately not executed here, specified for execution without this session's context.**
   - **V45/D12 feature-flag supersession** — executed as a **supersession**, per the forward plan's "complete file supersession" criterion: a retyped replacement lands Active at a path the V45/D12 ADR names; [feature-flag-lifecycle.md](../../doctrine/patterns/feature-flag-lifecycle.md) is stamped Deprecated → Superseded per lifecycle §§4–6 (that ADR also corrects the file's own cross-link note, which currently says "revision of this file").
   - **Historical banners** (editorial, no ADR, per lifecycle §1) on the superseded 2026-04 evolution-note candidates: the mythos-era note, research-ai-ml-ops-landscape-2026-04, research-enterprise-rag-agents-indexing-2026-04, research-internal-ai-knowledge-factory-governance-2026-04, deep-research-section-gaps, the public-doctrine benchmark/taxonomy pair, scorecard-vs-mainstream-frameworks, and moscow-review — each confirmed at execution, with live citations from tooling files repointed or marked as history links.
   - **Estate stubs** — [aws-container-runtimes.md](../../doctrine/tooling/estates/aws-container-runtimes.md) and [gcp-container-runtimes.md](../../doctrine/tooling/estates/gcp-container-runtimes.md) get a fill-or-retire decision at the first sweep.
   - **[platform-engineering.md](../../doctrine/principles/platform-engineering.md) disposition ADR** — candidate first file demotion; [platform-as-product-and-golden-paths.md](../../doctrine/patterns/platform-as-product-and-golden-paths.md) is the candidate merge home, with the disposition ADR free to choose otherwise with reasons.
   - **First sweep** — runs with the next minor release, scoped to its real work: the ADR 0021 decision and the estate-stub decision (the fade epoch is the `v0.5.0` tag, so no fade flag can arm before mid-2027). The 12-month staleness arm first arms at the first minor release after 2027-04 (the corpus is too young for a non-empty staleness cohort before then); that sweep is pre-planned as a council-review release so the April-cohort cliff is staffed, not rubber-stamped.
8. **Companion navigation (landing checklist).** docs/adr/README.md row; CHANGELOG `[Unreleased]` **Added** entry; doctrine/README.md pattern row, DEPRECATED.md pointer, and evolution-note row; [glossary.md](../../doctrine/glossary.md) entries for **Deprecated / Superseded / Retired / Rescinded (doctrine lifecycle)** and **tombstone**, plus a cross-link from the existing ADR entry to the new supersession mechanics; [how-to-read-this-doctrine.md](../../doctrine/patterns/how-to-read-this-doctrine.md)'s "Editing this library" enumeration gains the lifecycle pattern, and its layer overview notes the register as a root navigation surface; SITEMAP regeneration; SEMANTIC_INDEX entries; REFERENCES.md internal-map rows for the pattern, the register, and the evolution note. **ENGINEERING.md and the tldr are deliberately untouched** — the lifecycle governs the library's maintenance, not the constitution's content. The batch does not land without these.

## Alternatives Considered

### Extend versioning §6 in place, no new file

Rejected. §6 is a consumer-communication surface inside a pattern about compatibility signalling; the lifecycle is maintainer process with its own vocabulary, registers, and cadence. Overloading §6 would bury the machinery where adopters, not maintainers, read — and the audit's headline was precisely that §6's four bullets were the entire mechanism and went unused for five releases. The adopted split keeps §6 as the consumer surface (decision 3a) and homes the machinery in the pattern.

### A multi-tier graduation ladder (CNCF/TC39-style stages)

Rejected. Stage ladders are committee-shaped: each rung exists to meter multi-party consensus. At a single-operator repo the ADR is the whole quorum; three statuses plus three exits is the entire useful state space (IETF itself cut from three standards tiers to two on the same reasoning).

### Automatic time-based expiry

Rejected. Auto-expiry executes retirement without judgment and would silently break consumers pinned to old guidance. The adopted design uses time only to **force the question** (sweep and fade both flag; a human verdict is always the actor), never to execute the answer.

### Full front-matter status metadata on every file

Rejected for now. Stamping every Active file adds noise without information; Active-by-default with exception-only markers keeps banners rare and meaningful. Revisit if the corpus federates or grows automated consumers that need machine-readable status on every file.

## Consequences

**Positive:** demotion, supersession, retirement, and restoration become normal ADR-routed moves with a place to stand; the ambiguous removal gate is replaced by a tag-anchored window that works pre-1.0; the Deprecation change class becomes usable; the ADR index can express supersession and withdrawal; the stuck cases (feature-flag file, 2026-04 notes, estate stubs, platform-engineering) have named, executable routes; accretion meets two engines whose trigger lives in the release checklist and whose state lives in the register's sweep ledger; the library starts obeying its own house law §6.

**Costs and risks:** the sweep and register add per-release upkeep — mitigated by exception-only markers, cohort bounding, and the empty-register default. The 90-day/one-minor floor is a judgment call at pre-1.0 cadence; a sweep can lengthen it by ADR if consumers surface. The engines are themselves controls that can rot; the checklist trigger and sweep ledger exist precisely so a skipped engine is visible in release evidence rather than silent. Decision 7's routed applications are commitments of intent, not landed work — they appear as open items until executed.

## Consumer Impact

**Change class:** additive guidance (new pattern, register, evolution note) plus **normative tightening scoped to maintenance of this library** — obligations bind maintainers and change automation, not adopters. Two consumer-facing SHOULD-level signals land in versioning §6 (new adoption SHOULD NOT reference Deprecated surfaces; consumers SHOULD stop following Rescinded text): advisory signals about the library's own content, changing no adopter's compliance position. No existing file changes status in this batch.

**Compatibility proposal:** pre-1.0 minor; ships with `v0.5.0`.
