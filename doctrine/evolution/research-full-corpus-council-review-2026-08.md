# Research: Full-Corpus Council Review At v0.4.0 (August 2026)

**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Status:** Non-normative research note. Nothing here changes doctrine by itself — every fix routes through the forward plan ([post-v0.3.0 decisions and v0.4.0 plan](post-v0.3.0-external-review-decisions-and-v0.4.0-plan-2026-07.md), lanes V40–V45) or a new ADR.
**Scope:** The full corpus at tag `v0.4.0`.

---

## 1. Purpose And Method

This is the first **full-corpus council review** since the v0.4.0 surge (ADRs 0031–0035, gap-audit set G1–G13/S1–S5 closed). Method:

- **Seven area reviews**, each a full-file read of its slice against a common rubric (contradictions, duplicate normative homes, untyped material claims, staleness, portability leaks, checklist coverage, usability).
- **Three cross-cutting lenses** run over the whole corpus: cross-file contradictions, normative discipline, and reader experience.
- **Adversarial verification of every blocker and major**: each candidate finding was independently re-derived from the files (both sides reproduced, refutation conditions tested — non-overlapping activation, typed-parent inheritance). Findings that failed verification were dropped; several were corrected in scope or provenance during verification and are reported in corrected form.
- **Minors are carried as reviewer-reported** — flagged, plausible, not adversarially verified.

The result: **2 confirmed blockers, 20 confirmed majors, 31 reviewer-reported minors.** No finding below is speculative; every confirmed item cites reproduced text.

### Blockers

**B1 — Feature-flag FSM has no coherent rollback path** ([feature-flag-lifecycle.md](../patterns/feature-flag-lifecycle.md) §2, lines 44–45). "Flags do not move backwards" is immediately followed by "the flag returns to `active` with a lower percentage" — a backwards move from stabilize/cleanup — while the next bullet forbids targeting changes in stabilize/cleanup. Verification confirmed no reading composes all three sentences: stabilize lasts a week, §7 concedes regressions surface subtly during it, and the prescribed percentage reduction is a targeting change the freeze forbids. The suggested remedy (create a new ops flag) cannot gate the original flag's already-deployed code without a redeploy, contradicting §7's tested pre-GA rollback requirement. This matters because it is the corpus's only unresolvable instruction set: an operator reversing a bad rollout after stabilize **cannot comply**. Fix: permit `stabilize → active` as the one recorded-reason reversal (restarting the stabilize clock on re-promotion), scope the targeting freeze to `cleanup` with an incident-review escape, delete the unqualified no-backwards sentence and the new-flag remedy. **Owner: the V45/D12 feature-flag supersession ADR** — this rewrite should land inside it, alongside M12–M14.

**B2 — Slow-burn alert queries series that are never defined** ([observability.md](../principles/observability.md) §6.3). The recording-rules block defines only `job:request_error_ratio:rate1h` and `:rate5m`; the `ErrorBudgetBurnSlow` alert queries `:rate3d` and `:rate6h`, defined nowhere in the repo. Prometheus evaluates missing series as an empty vector without error, so a consumer copy-pasting the YAML deploys a slow-burn alert that **silently never fires** — precisely the "silent budget exhaustion" failure §6.4 warns against, and the adaptation guidance never says to author the missing rules. Compounding: all four aggregations use bare `sum(rate(...))`, dropping the `job` label, so the `job:` naming prefix is wrong, `{{ $labels.job }}` renders empty, and multi-job estates blend error ratios across services. This matters because example code that misleads is worse than no example in a doctrine corpus that trades on evidence discipline. Fix: add `rate3d`/`rate6h` recording rules and change every aggregation to `sum by (job) (rate(...))`. **Owner: fold into the M20 relocation change (new small ADR or editorial fix under the doctrine-change harness)** — fix the YAML and move it in one pass.

### Majors

**M1 — Duplicated human-oversight duties.** ai-ml-systems §4 (line 59) and ai-adoption-controls §2 (line 43) each enumerate the identical four runtime oversight duties (designed fallback; overseer disregard/override/halt authority; contest path; per-decision logging) in full normative voice — the "Detail:" pointer does not make one a summary, and the pattern copy has already diverged (logging field list, broader activation scope). Every other cross-reference in the pair is a compressed trigger plus pointer; §4 is the sole full re-enumeration. Fix: make adoption-controls §2 canonical (it carries the richer text and the §1 inventory points there); reduce the §4 bullet to trigger-plus-pointer with duty names as a parenthetical index only. **Route: canonical-home consolidation ADR (new; see §6).**

**M2 — Duplicated materiality-axis rule.** ai-ml-systems §2.1 and ai-adoption-controls §1.1 state the same max(capability, materiality) scaling rule in near-verbatim prose with identical examples, and the predicted drift has already happened: §1.1 carries the UK PS21/3 impact-tolerance-inheritance clause, §2.1 lacks it. Fix: §1.1 canonical; shrink §2.1 to axis declaration plus link, explicitly **retaining §2.1's unique clarification that the §2 tier table stays capability-keyed**. Route: same consolidation ADR as M1.

**M3 — Payment-authority MUST has no landing surface.** cost-and-finops §7.1 mandates recording any payment/transaction-authority grant "in the AI inventory (ai-adoption-controls §1)" — but the §1 field table defines exactly eight fields with no financial-authority field and no extensibility language, and a grep for payment/transaction across the pattern returns zero matches; neither checklist elicits it. A consumer maintaining the inventory strictly from the schema will never record the grant, defeating the per-agent revocation rationale §7.1 itself gives. Fix: add a **Financial / transaction authority** row to the §1 table (default none; grant record link, approver, caps, expiry, dedicated principal) and a default-deny item to ai-adoption-readiness. **Route: checklist/derivation reconciliation lane (new; see §6).**

**M4 — Checklist lags the §3 test matrix.** ai-adoption-readiness's Harm-Surface Testing block has no item for Tier-D agent-hijack/tool-flow-abuse adaptive evaluation (ASI01/ASI02, attacker-iterates bar) and none for the synthetic-media re-test cadence — both normative in the pattern the checklist claims to align with. Provenance correction from verification: attribute to **ADR 0035 (245249d) and the ADR 0023 audit-fix commit (7f107cc), not ADR 0033**. Fix: two one-line items. Route: reconciliation lane.

**M5 — Untyped FinOps thresholds.** cost-and-finops §2 states a mandatory five-key tag schema at provisioning, >85%/60-day and >90% coverage targets, and ">10% is a process failure" entirely in bold imperative prose — no keyword, no activation condition, no exception route — while §7.1 in the same file shows the corrected style. Fix per verification: MUST for tagging-at-provisioning **only with an explicit estate-governed-cloud scope** (else SHOULD); the five literal key names become EXAMPLE; the numbers become CONTEXT-DEPENDENT defaults with exception-contract departure. **Route: V41 (classify material BCP-14 claims).**

The governed-execution overlay's activation clause explicitly includes "mutates persistent state", so this is inside its declared scope. Fix: one composite overlay item covering admission mode, TTL, forget, relinquishment, recovery, and the vendor-platform compensating control; optionally a memory-poisoning line in harm-surface testing. Route: reconciliation lane.

## 5. Systemic Observations

Synthesising across the seven areas and three lenses:

**The corpus is coherent — but its coherence is now concentrated in the new stratum.** The v0.4.0 surge landed cleanly by the crosscut evidence: single-canonical-owner discipline mostly held (seven of nine sampled multi-file rules have one unambiguous home), the five-file agent identity chain is mutually consistent across independently-landed batches, and budget enforcement is uniformly placed outside the prompt. Where the corpus fails, it fails **at the seams between generations**, not inside either generation. Every area review independently reported the same fracture: a typed, activation-scoped, exception-routed post-ADR-0028 stratum sitting inside or beside an untyped bold-imperative legacy stratum. The normative-discipline lens quantified it — roughly 25 principle/pattern files carry material obligations with zero capitalised keywords, including the most load-bearing anchors (merge-path §2 invariants, cited as binding by typed rules elsewhere; build.md's 20 lowercase must/should; feature-flag hard rules). ADR 0032 deferred the retrofit with no owner, no trigger, and no backlog — violating the house law's own §6 lifecycle requirement — so absent action the ambiguity is permanent and **compounds**: every new typed rule that composes with an untyped anchor (M11's §6 deferring to an untyped "Humans approve") inherits the ambiguity.

**Normative concentration.** By the reviews' own evidence, the heaviest typed-MUST density sits in the AI corpus: ai-ml-systems (§§4, 5, 7), ai-adoption-controls (§§1–5), cost-and-finops §7.1, zero-trust §2.1, testing-strategy §5, dependencies-supply-chain §8, agentic-loop-design (§§5.2, 8.2, 9.4, 10), privacy §5.4, and the run-contract/verifier-pack schemas. The classic corpus's obligations are at least as strong in intent (secrets, merge-path, signing) but formally weaker in expression — the inversion is starkest in deps, where model weights carry typed MUSTs that the release binaries they extend do not (M18). The AI/classic balance is therefore right in **substance** and wrong in **register**: the corpus does not over-govern AI; it under-formalises everything else.

**One genuine doctrinal contradiction is open, not just stylistic drift**: two authority models for machine verdicts (pre-v0.4.0 "a model output is not merge authority" absolutes vs v0.4.0 calibrated judge-gated promotion) coexist with no precedence rule. This is the only confirmed seam where an estate can adopt the new stratum and thereby silently read down the old one.

**The reader-experience lens delivers the sharpest verdict on adoption**: the content layer outran the navigation layer. The first-hour path still works (ENGINEERING.md → tldr held compact under growth), but beyond it, five batches of binding agent doctrine landed across ~10 files while README omits four first-class files, no semantic route reaches cost-and-finops, and the checklists collect almost none of ADRs 0031–0035 (M3, M4, M6). A Tier-D team following the published routes today silently misses the financial default-deny, agent identity floor, GenAI telemetry, and adaptive-evaluation duties — and falls back on the ASI crosswalk as an obligation index, a role it explicitly disclaims. The corpus's derivation rule ("no obligation exists only in a checklist") was honoured; its converse — obligations must *reach* the checklists — was not enforced by anything.

## 7. What Is Genuinely Strong

Grounded strictly in reviewer evidence, this corpus does several things its public peers do not:

- **A working meta-governance layer.** The normative-language / revision-pinned-profiles / doctrine-versioning trio is concrete, typed, and mutually consistent — claim anatomy, exception admissibility, and pinning invariants that most public engineering handbooks simply do not have. Notably, most of this review's majors were found **by applying the corpus's own rules to itself**; the house law is sharp enough to cut its author.
- **Honest gap declaration as a norm.** "Honest v1 Gap" sections in the contracts trio, per-row "No current coverage" entries in the ASI crosswalk, "no ratified SLSA ML track" framing in deps §8, and the ADR index's retrospective-honesty rule mean a security reviewer can trust coverage statements as coverage statements.
- **Evidence-typed obligations.** Material claims arrive with quantitative citations and minimum-viable-evidence bars (testing-strategy §5's 11%→81% AISI and >90% adaptive-bypass figures; agentic-loop judge discipline and memory red-teaming) rather than assertion.
- **End-to-end decision traceability.** Gap-audit IDs (G1–G13/S1–S5) threaded through CHANGELOG, ADRs 0031–0035, and evolution notes make every normative addition auditable back to its evidence — the review could date and attribute individual checklist-drift events from git history alone.
- **A coherent, non-forked agent governance model.** The two-axis tier × materiality model, the identity/attribution chain consistent across five files landed in separate batches, uniform outside-the-prompt budget enforcement, and incident agent-responders that compose with existing doctrine instead of inventing a parallel autonomy framework.
- **The principle/tooling split executed properly at least once** (observability §7 vs tooling GenAI attributes) — proving the layering is achievable, which is what makes B2/M20 fixable defects rather than architectural flaws.
- **Falsifiable prompts over vocabulary.** Threat-modeling §4's blast-radius acceptance questions and the burn-rate failure-mode table convert slogans into reviewer-checkable questions — rare in doctrine corpora.
- **A first-hour path that survived growth.** ENGINEERING.md holding all AI content to one proposition plus one route row, and a 51-line tldr, kept the entry compact through five ADRs of additions.

The honest summary: the corpus's failure modes are those of a system with real standards applied unevenly across time — drift, lag, and register split — not those of a system without standards. Every confirmed finding has a mechanical fix and an obvious owning lane; none required inventing new doctrine machinery, because the machinery already exists.
