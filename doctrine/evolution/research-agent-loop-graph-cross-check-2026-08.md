# Research: Agent, Loop, And Graph Coverage Cross-Check (August 2026)

**Status:** research note (non-normative). **Date:** 2026-08-06. **Method:** two passes. **Pass 1 (coverage):** a circulating practitioner article ("Agents, Loops, Graphs — Everything You Need to Know in One Place", @Mahaximus_ on X, 2026-08-05) was decomposed into twelve checkable claims and each mapped against the corpus by a coverage agent with file-and-line verification. **Pass 2 (evidence):** three deep-research subagents built the multi-source ledger in §5 — (i) graph/DAG decomposition and orchestration, (ii) iteration loops, stop conditions, and economics, (iii) agent definitions, autonomy leveling, and automation-selection criteria — grading every source by authority class and marking claims multiply-attested / single-source / unattested. **Source authority and limits:** the article is informal practitioner content used only as a **coverage foil**; the §4 action rows rest on the §5 ledger, not on the article. Proposals route via ADR per repo convention ([../../docs/adr/](../../docs/adr/)).

---

## 1. Purpose and method

Popular practitioner material is a cheap adversary: if a widely-shared explainer teaches a technique the corpus cannot answer, that is a coverage signal worth recording regardless of the source's rigor. The article covers three escalating structures — agents (goal + tools + memory + loop), loops (self-checking iteration with stop conditions), and graphs (DAG decomposition with parallel workers and an independent checker). Each claim was mapped to a verdict: **covered** (with normative level), **partial**, **not covered**, or **known gap** (the corpus already records it as open). The gaps that survived were then researched against primary and canonical sources so the resulting action rows carry a researched basis per the change harness, not a single social-media citation.

Headline: **the corpus is covered — and usually stricter — on agents and loops; graphs are half-covered**, with governance of graphs normative but the design method absent, and the graph primitive itself a recorded run-contracts v2 gap with no lane or owner. The deep-research pass additionally corrected two of the article's claims outright (§3 items 2 and 4) and one assumption in this note's own first draft (memory is not definitional to agenthood — §4 row 5).

## 3. Divergences — where the article's advice would violate doctrine

Recorded so the foil is not silently half-adopted; no action rows arise from these. Ledger references in brackets.

1. **"Typed output so the next node consumes it without a human."** Doctrine: "a typed shape prevents ambiguity; it does not make the content true" (agentic-loop §8.1); handoffs are checked before integration (§8.3). Externally corroborated: vendor structured-output docs themselves guarantee structure, not semantics [G4.1–G4.2], and schema-valid-but-semantically-wrong is the dominant residual failure mode [G4.4]. The literal reading licenses the context-poisoning-via-chained-outputs anti-pattern (§12).
2. **"The checker MUST have a completely fresh context."** Doctrine deliberately replaced structural separation with **failure-mode diversity** (ai-native SDLC §11; [honest-review-synthesis.md](honest-review-synthesis.md) records the replacement decision). The self-correction literature grounds this harder than the article's own rule: intrinsic self-correction *degrades* performance and the fix is a reliable **external** signal, not a fresh window on the same model [L1.3, L1.4]. Fresh context is a context-engineering device, not an independence guarantee — and the article omits any duty to *validate the checker*, MUST-level for load-bearing judges (agentic-loop §8.2).
3. **"After 8 attempts stop and report" as a prompt instruction.** Non-conforming: ceilings are enforced in host/workflow policy, outside prompts, recorded in receipts (§8.3). Shipped frameworks agree — the ceiling is a harness parameter (LangGraph `recursion_limit`, OpenAI SDK `max_turns`, Claude Code's stop-hook override), never a prompt line [L3.1–L3.4].
4. **"Below ~50% keep rate the loop costs more than manual."** A portable numeric threshold with **no published basis** (searched directly; none exists — L5.7). The published record: 44% agent-code survival into commits [L5.2, single-source preprint]; a randomized trial where AI-assisted developers were 19% slower while self-estimating 20% faster [L5.6] — i.e., net-negative loops occur and self-perception cannot be the stop signal, but no universal break-even number is attested, and suggestion-level data shows ~27% acceptance coexisting with perceived gains [L5.1]. Corpus policy independently keeps such numbers estate-set (ADR 0034).
5. **The article's loop-worthiness list omits blast radius/materiality entirely** — its four criteria would green-light a self-grading loop that moves money; doctrine holds that at approve-before-irreversible (§6) or default-deny (cost-and-finops §7.1) regardless.
6. **One autonomy axis where doctrine has three** (capability tier, materiality, oversight position). The field's best sources agree with doctrine: autonomy level is "a deliberate design decision, separate from capability" [A2.2]; SAE J3016 levels are responsibility-division, not capability [A2.1]. The taxonomies that conflate the axes [A2.3, A2.4] are the pattern doctrine warns against.
7. **No injection defence anywhere in the article** — architectural defence is non-optional for Tier D (agentic-loop §9); an article-shaped agent reading external content with write tools is under-specified against doctrine.
