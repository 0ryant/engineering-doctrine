# Research: Target-State Definition And Irreversible Decisions (August 2026)

**Status:** research note (non-normative). **Date:** 2026-08-12. **Method:** three passes. **Pass 1 (coverage):** an operator proposition — *"we are missing 'start with the end in mind'; knowing the target state from day 0 surfaces gotchas and gaps early"* — was decomposed into ten checkable claims and each mapped against the corpus by grep-and-read with file-and-line verification. **Pass 2 (evidence):** the surviving gaps were researched against primary and canonical sources across six clusters — target-state definition, decision timing and reversibility, retrofit economics, quality attributes and design-time legal duties, exit and end-of-life, and gap surfacing — grading every source by authority class and marking claims multiply-attested / single-source / contested / unattested. **Pass 3 (diligence, same day):** every ledger row re-verified against its primary where reachable — six primaries read in full via local text extraction (Bezos 2015 letter, Fowler 2003, Boehm & Basili 2001, ATAM TR, Klein 2007, EDPB Guidelines 4/2019) and eleven more fetched directly; the pass corrected three pass-1 coverage claims, two citation misattributions, one overly-strong verified negative, one gate-ordering error, and three authority-class labels (log at end of §5). **Pass 4 (council ratification, same day):** three-critic council — attestation, corpus-claims, red-team — unanimous RATIFY-WITH-FIXES; the applied fixes (headline narrowed to match the pass-3 evidence, gap-#4 adjacency, prop-9 re-grounding, further class-label tightening, CHANGELOG companion) are recorded after the §5 diligence log. **Source authority and limits:** the operator proposition is an informal prompt used only as a **coverage foil**; the §4 action rows rest on the §5 ledger. The pass-2 evidence **forced two changes to this note's own first framing** (§3 items 2 and 5). Proposals route via ADR per repo convention ([../../docs/adr/](../../docs/adr/)).

## 1. Purpose and method

The corpus governs changes — and, increasingly, its operating assets — well; what it does not govern is the system's own end shape. Every gate in [ai-native-software-development-lifecycle.md](../patterns/ai-native-software-development-lifecycle.md) is scoped to a **change**: G1 admits a mandate (why this work exists), G2 types claims about the proposed change and binds them to a **candidate**. No file owns the question *what must be true of this system when it is finished* — the target operating shape against which today's decisions are either cheap or ruinous. The foil asserts that stating it early surfaces gaps early.

Each claim was mapped to a verdict: **covered** (with normative level), **partial**, **not covered**, or **known gap** (already recorded as open). Surviving gaps were then researched so the action rows carry a graded basis rather than an appeal to a management aphorism.

Headline: **the gap is real, narrower than first framed, and the corpus holds the brake without the accelerator.** It has strong, repeated doctrine against premature construction — speculative-complexity anti-patterns, choose-boring-technology, "no universal four-layer architecture" — and its existing irreversibility doctrine is **enactment-scoped** (approve-before-irreversible routes the action at execution time) or **asset-scoped** (provider exit, dependency substitution, agent-identity decommissioning): for the system itself there is no design-time discipline for the decisions whose option value expires at inception. The evidence also **disqualifies the usual justification**: the cost-of-change curve cannot carry this argument (§3 item 2), and the defensible basis is structural, not statistical. The reconciliation that survived contact with the sources is not "decide early" but **decide at the moment the alternative disappears** — which for a small, identifiable class of decisions is day 0, and for everything else is not.

## 3. Divergences — where the naive framing would violate doctrine

Recorded so the foil is not silently half-adopted. Ledger references in brackets. Items 2 and 5 corrected this note's own first draft.

1. **"Define the target state up front, then build to it."** Waterfall-shaped, and the corpus rejects it explicitly (SDLC §1 non-goals; anti-pattern class 2). Fowler's scope rule is the discriminator and it is narrower than the foil: "Yagni only applies to capabilities built into the software to support a presumptive feature, it does not apply to effort to make the software easier to modify" [D3.1]. Adoptable residue: state the target, build nothing extra for it.

2. **"Retrofitting costs 10–100× more, therefore decide early."** *(Corrects this note's first draft, which reached for Boehm.)* The canonical curve does not support a portable claim. Boehm & Basili's own paper — an IEEE *Computer* magazine piece, not a peer-reviewed study, which is itself part of the provenance problem — puts the escalation at **5:1 for small, noncritical projects** against ~100:1 for large critical ones [R1.1], i.e. the multiplier is a function of system materiality, not a constant. The widely-circulated 1×/6.5×/15×/100× table is separately argued to lack an empirical basis adequate to its fame [R1.3, R1.5]. Doctrine already refuses exactly this class of number. **The defensible basis is structural, not statistical**: some properties are enforced at every call site and in the shape of stored data, so changing them later is a data migration plus an exhaustive sweep with a silent-failure mode — the missed `WHERE tenant_id` is a disclosure, not a bug [R2.1, R2.2]. Google SRE states the qualitative form without a number: "While design decisions can be reversed or rectified later in the development lifecycle, such changes come at a high cost in terms of effort and complexity" [T3.1]. Any adopted rule should carry the structural argument and no multiplier.

3. **"The target state is the plan."** Collides with Core Proposition 3 and SDLC invariant 11 ("enactment is not completion"; reality can falsify the plan). A target-state record that cannot be revised becomes the frozen-requirements failure the corpus already avoids. Adoptable residue: a **versioned record with an owner and a review trigger**, using the §8 lifecycle shape (§2 claim 9).

4. **"Surface every gap before starting."** Unbounded, and inverts the corpus's proportionality rule. The bound the sources supply is the **decision horizon**, not completeness: surface the gaps that bear on decisions whose alternatives expire now.

5. **"Defer decisions to the last responsible moment" is the opposing principle.** *(Corrects this note's first draft, which treated LRM as the counterweight.)* Under Poppendieck's own definition it is the **same rule** — though the deferral bias in the principle as commonly taught is real, and is exactly what Wirfs-Brock's dissent targets. Poppendieck defines the last responsible moment as "the moment at which failing to make a decision eliminates an important alternative", and notes that commitments delayed past it "are made by default" [D2.1, D2.2]. A one-way door is precisely a decision whose last responsible moment arrives at inception. Wirfs-Brock sharpens this against architecture specifically — decisions ripple beyond their apparent scope, early implementation sets unintended precedent, and deferral "may put others in a bind" — proposing the **"most responsible moment"** instead [D2.3]. The corpus needs the arrival-time question, not a new bias toward early or late.

6. **The premortem's "30%" improvement.** The HBR piece (read in full) attributes it to a single 1989 study — "Research conducted in 1989 by Deborah J. Mitchell, of the Wharton School; Jay Russo, of Cornell; and Nancy Pennington, of the University of Colorado" [U1.1]; the underlying study was not independently examined here. Adopt the **technique** (prospective hindsight: assume the failure has happened and ask what caused it), never the figure — same discipline as item 2.

7. **"Start with the end in mind" as the name.** Covey's habit 2 (*The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*, 1989) is a self-help framing with no engineering evidence base; the researched content is Amazon's working-backwards narrative [T1.1, T1.2], SEI quality-attribute scenarios [T2.1, T2.4], and SRE early engagement [T3.1, T3.2]. If adopted, name the mechanism, not the aphorism.
